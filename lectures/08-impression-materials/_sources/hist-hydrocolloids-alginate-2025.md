I. Introduction
  • Impression material is a substance used for making an accurate replica of
    the hard and soft oral tissues by registering the form and relationship of
    the teeth and the surrounding oral tissues.
  • The impression material is introduced to the mouth in an unset condition
    and is maintained in position against the oral tissues until it set, and then
    it is removed from the mouth.
  • The set impression is a negative reproduction of the oral tissues. To obtain
    a positive reproduction; the set impression is poured by a suitable model
    or die material.

Alginate impression material: composition
     1. Sodium Potassium Alginate – used as a thickening agent (~14%).
     2. Calcium Sulfate – reacts with the potassium alginate to form the gel (~16%)
     3. Trisodium phosphate – added to slow down the reaction time for mixing
       (~2%)
     4. Diatomaceous Earth – filler that adds bulk to the material (~60%)
     5. Potassium Sulfate or Fluoride
     6. Glycol (in dustless alginates)
Alginate impression material: setting reaction
     2 Na2PO4 + 3 CaSO4
                                  3 Na2SO4 + Ca3(PO4)
     Sodium alginate + CaSO4 + H2O
                                        Ca alginate + Na2SO4
Alginate impression material: setting reaction
     1. Sodium phosphate reacts with calcium phosphate (working
        time)
     2. After sodium phosphate has reacted, the remaining calcium
        phosphate react with sodium alginate
     3. Formation of calcium alginate (gel)
Alginate impression material: advantages
      1. Easy of mixing and manipulation
      2. Minimum equipment
      3. Flexible
      4. Accuracy
      5. Low cost
Alginate impression material: disadvantages
      1. Restrict the model & die material to only gypsum.
      2. Not accurate in reproducing fine details (compared
         with elastomeric impression materials)
      3. Poor dimensional stability
      4. They are messy to work with and cannot be
         corrected
