# Sailer et al. 2018: Survival and complication rates of zirconia-ceramic vs metal-ceramic multiple-unit FDPs (systematic review)

Page convention: the journal pages printed on the article are 184–198. PDF page = journal page − 183. All citations below are **journal pages**.

**Scope warning before anything else.** This review covers **implant-supported** multiple-unit FDPs only. The abstract, PICO and all data are implant-supported (p.184–185). It is **not** about tooth-supported crowns. The lecture should not use its survival numbers as teaching content; they appear here only to frame caveats.

## 2. Evidence type and design

- **Type:** systematic review and meta-analysis of clinical studies.
- **Inclusion (p.186–187):**
  - RCTs, prospective and retrospective cohorts/case series.
  - At least 10 patients and a mean follow-up of at least 3 years.
  - Patients examined clinically.
  - Restorations either veneered zirconia frameworks or monolithic zirconia, compared with metal-ceramic.
  - Pooled-material studies only if more than 90% of restorations were in one group.
  - Excluded: more than 15% full-arch restorations, in vitro, animal and preclinical studies.
- **Search (p.185–186):**
  - MEDLINE (PubMed) up to and including November 2016, plus hand search. No language restriction.
  - 5,263 titles → 455 abstracts → 240 full texts → 197 excluded → 43 included (35 for single crowns, analysed in a separate review) → **19 studies on FDPs** (Fig. 1, p.186; p.187, p.190).
- **Included (p.190):**
  - 16 metal-ceramic studies and 3 zirconia-ceramic studies.
  - **No RCT compared the two materials.**
  - **No study on monolithic zirconia met the criteria.**
  - Zirconia studies published 2010 and 2014 (×2) (p.190). The 2015 Worni study is listed in Tables 2 and 4.
- **Numbers:**
  - Survival analysis: 932 metal-ceramic FDPs (mean follow-up 6.3 y) and **175 zirconia FDPs** (mean 5.1 y) (p.190; Table 5 p.193).
  - Metal-ceramic: 993 restorations on 2,289 implants, 73% cement-retained (p.190).
  - Zirconia: 175 restorations, 15% cemented and 85% screw-retained (p.190).
  - Unit span: metal-ceramic 2–6 units; zirconia 3–5 units, one study up to 12 units (p.190).
- **Statistics (p.189–190):**
  - Event rate = events / total FDP exposure time. Poisson regression with robust SEs.
  - 5-year survival S(T) = exp(−T × event rate), assuming constant event rates.
  - Spearman goodness-of-fit for heterogeneity. Multivariable Poisson regression for material and design.
  - Stata 12.1. PRISMA 2009, except that **no formal quality assessment** was done.
- **Definitions (p.189–190):**
  - Survival = FDP remaining in situ with or without modification.
  - Success = free of all complications.
  - Mechanical complications include ceramic fractures and chippings (p.187).

## 3. Mechanistic statements relevant to the lecture

This review is clinical. It contains **very little material-science explanation**. Every mechanistic statement it makes is listed below.

- "In general, **bilayer materials are prone to delamination or chipping** as the material scientific research has shown (Zhang, Sailer, & Lawn, 2013)" (p.194). **CITED** (Zhang Y, Sailer I, Lawn BR. Fatigue of dental ceramics. J Dent 2013;41:1135–1147, as listed on p.198).
- "One possible, interesting alternative to bilayers is the application of monolithic types of reconstructions (Zhang et al., 2013)" (p.194). **PROPOSED.**
- "A few years ago, this was not possible with zirconia materials, as the aesthetics of the yttria-stabilized zirconia used for FDP framework fabrication was too poor" (p.194). This is the authors' explanation of why veneering was needed.
- "More recently, however, new more translucent and/or colored new types of zirconia ceramics were introduced reducing the need for veneering ceramic. Monolithic zirconia reconstructions … may exhibit lower rates of chipping of the ceramic. The literature on this topic is still scarce" (p.195). **PROPOSED** (not tested: no monolithic studies were included).
- "Further developments of the zirconia veneering ceramics and of the veneering procedures have helped lower the initially high incidences of chipping, still, the problem remains to be the predominant technical complication" (p.194). **Asserted; no specific development is named.**
- Failure modes (p.194): "Frequent reason for failure was a **catastrophic fracture of the zirconia framework** itself. Another often observed reason for failure was **extended chipping of the veneering ceramic** (Sailer et al., 2017)."
- "The predominant technical complication at both types of FDPs was chipping and/or fracture of the veneering ceramic. This complication was more often observed at the zirconia-ceramic FDPs and, in addition, significantly more often led to loss of the FDP" (p.194).

**Bottom line for the lecture.** This paper can support the *existence* of the clinical problem (chipping is the predominant technical complication of veneered zirconia, more frequent and more often leading to loss than with metal-ceramic, p.194). It also gives the general bilayer argument (p.194). For CTE mismatch, residual stress, cooling rate/thermal conductivity and framework support, **another source is needed.** The review points to Zhang, Sailer & Lawn 2013 (J Dent 41:1135–1147) as its material-science reference (p.194, p.198). That paper was **not** read here, so nothing is claimed about its content.

## 6. Controversies and caveats

- **Implant-supported only.** Extrapolating to tooth-supported FDPs relies on the authors' remark that tooth-supported reviews "demonstrated similar outcomes" (Heintze & Rousson 2010; Schley et al. 2010) (p.195).
- **Highly unbalanced evidence.**
  - 932 metal-ceramic vs 175 zirconia FDPs; 16 vs 3 studies (p.190, p.195).
  - The 50% chipping figure comes from **one study of 13 FDPs** (p.184, p.192).
  - No RCT compared the two materials (p.190, p.195).
- **Chipping definitions were not standardised.** "The incidence of ceramic fractures or chippings was not reported in a standardized way and changed significantly depending on the definition utilized" (p.192). Rates are therefore definition-dependent.
- **Older zirconia and veneering technology.** The zirconia studies date from 2010–2015, and the authors note that veneering ceramics and procedures improved later (p.194).
- **Monolithic zirconia not represented.** The conclusions apply to veneered zirconia only (p.195).
- **Confounding by retention type.** 85% of zirconia FDPs were screw-retained vs 73% of metal-ceramic cemented (p.190). The review does not analyse this.
- **Search and quality.** MEDLINE only, with the authors' justification given (p.195–196). No formal quality assessment (p.189–190).
- **Internal inconsistencies.**
  - Zirconia FDPs lost due to fracture: 4.1% (abstract p.184, text p.192) vs 4.7% (1.9–11.7%) in Table 6 (p.196). The figure 4.1% also appears in Table 6 as metal-ceramic screw loosening.
  - Worni 2015: "Total number of included FDPs 89" but "Examined FDPs in situ at end of observation 91" (Table 4, p.192).
  - Prospective (11 studies, 710 FDPs) + retrospective (6 studies, 397 FDPs) = 17 studies in the design sub-analysis (p.190), whereas 19 studies are included.
- **Lab vs clinic:** no laboratory data here. Clinical outcomes combine material, design, technician and patient factors. The review cannot attribute chipping to any specific material-science cause.

## 7. Use in lecture

- **Section 2 (veneering/chipping), one caveat line only:**
  - Chipping of the veneering ceramic is the predominant technical complication of veneered zirconia multi-unit FDPs. It is reported more often than for metal-ceramic and more often leads to loss of the prosthesis (Sailer et al. 2018, p.194, implant-supported FDPs).
  - Present this as *why the materials science matters*, not as a survival statistic.
- **Section 2/4, conceptual statement:** "bilayer materials are prone to delamination or chipping" (p.194, citing Zhang, Sailer & Lawn 2013). This is the bridge to the material-science explanation, which must come from other sources: CTE mismatch, residual stress, cooling rate, framework support.
- **Section 4, historical-reasoning slide:**
  - Opaque 3Y zirconia needed veneering for aesthetics (p.194).
  - More translucent and coloured zirconias reduce the need for veneer, so monolithic zirconia "may" chip less (p.195).
  - Good structure→property→performance chain: translucency (microstructure/phase) leads to architecture choice, which leads to failure mode.
- **Section 4 or 5, failure-mode contrast:** the reasons for loss were catastrophic framework fracture or extended veneer chipping (p.194). Links to fatigue of bilayers (Section 5).
- **Critical-appraisal mini-case (Section 6 or the "evaluate claims" outcome):**
  - "50% chipping" comes from 13 FDPs in one study, with non-standardised definitions (p.184, p.192).
  - Teach students to ask: how many, how defined, which architecture, how old the technology?
- **Do not** put survival percentages (98.7% vs 93.0%) on student-facing slides. They are out of scope and implant-specific.
- **Source needed:** for CTE mismatch, residual stress, slow cooling and anatomic framework support, this review gives **nothing**. Find a primary or review source (e.g. Zhang, Sailer & Lawn 2013, J Dent 41:1135–1147, as cited on p.198; not yet read).
